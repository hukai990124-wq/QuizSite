### Part 5

**101. D** — Why: The gap is followed by a noun (presentation) that belongs to the speaker, so the possessive relative pronoun “whose” is needed; “who” and “whom” cannot come directly before a noun like this.

**102. D** — Why: The two clauses contrast (late delivery vs. launch on schedule), so the concessive conjunction “Although” fits; “Because” would wrongly present the delay as the reason for the on-time launch.

**103. A** — Why: People who feel an emotion are described with the -ed participle, so “pleased” is correct; “pleasing” describes the thing that causes the feeling.

**104. A** — Why: After “to” in “asked … to ___ the layout”, a base-form verb that takes an object is required, so “finalize” is correct; the other choices are an adjective, an adverb and a noun.

**105. C** — Why: The sentence presents two alternative ways to pay, so “or” is correct; “nor” requires a negative such as “neither”, and “but” and “so” do not express alternatives.

**106. B** — Why: The subject is the singular noun “list” (not “suppliers”), so the singular verb “is” is needed; “are” is the trap that agrees with the nearer plural noun.

**107. D** — Why: The pair is joined by “and”, which goes with “both”; “either” pairs with “or” and “neither” with “nor”.

**108. D** — Why: Inspections do not conduct anything; they are conducted by an engineer, so the passive “be conducted” is required after “must”.

**109. A** — Why: After “to” (and the adverb “significantly”) a verb taking the object “the time” is needed, so “shorten” is correct.
