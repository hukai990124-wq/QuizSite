### Part 5

**101. B** — Why: “Beside” means “next to” and correctly locates the desk in relation to one entrance; “between” needs two points of reference, “among” needs a group, and “throughout” means “in every part of”.

**102. D** — Why: Days and dates take “on” (on Friday, 14 March); “at” is used for clock times such as 5:00 P.M., which is the trap, and “in” is used for months and years.

**103. C** — Why: “Since the start of the year” describes a period that began in the past and continues to now, so the present perfect “has increased” is needed; “increasing” is not a finite verb.

**104. D** — Why: “Worked tirelessly” (worked very hard without stopping) is a natural collocation that fits a deadline context; “narrowly”, “vaguely” and “sparsely” do not describe how someone works toward a goal.

**105. B** — Why: The first clause gives the reason for the second (the hall was full, so a second room was arranged), so “Because” is correct; “Although” would wrongly signal a contrast.

**106. A** — Why: The subject is “Each”, which is singular, so the singular verb “includes” is required; “include” agrees with the nearby plural “packages”, which is the trap.

**107. B** — Why: Complaints do not forward anything; they are sent by someone, so the passive “are forwarded” is needed. The active forms “forward” and “have forwarded” would need an object.

**108. B** — Why: After the possessive “her”, a noun is required, so “appreciation” is correct; the other choices are a verb, an adjective and an adverb.

**109. D** — Why: The crops belong to the farmers, so the possessive relative pronoun “whose” is needed before the noun “crops”; “who”, “whom” and “which” cannot introduce a noun in this way.

**110. A** — Why: Rising fuel costs are the cause of the price increase, so “Due to” is correct; “In spite of” would suggest the price rose despite the costs, which is illogical.

**111. B** — Why: “Thoroughly tested” (tested completely and carefully) is a standard collocation; “shortly” means “soon” and does not describe how testing is done.

**112. A** — Why: The pair is completed by “or”, so “either” is needed; “neither” pairs with “nor”, “both” with “and”, and “not only” with “but also”.

**113. B** — Why: “Of all the models” and “the” signal a comparison among more than two items, so the superlative “greatest” is required; “greater” compares only two.

**119. C** — Why: The singular noun “employee” and the singular verb “must complete” require “Every”; “All”, “Few” and “Most” are used with plural countable nouns.
